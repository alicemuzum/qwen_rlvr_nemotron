53 gravity / unit_conversion rows are graded wrong — for two separate reasons

Re-grading those two categories with the repo's own compare_answer() moves gravity 113 → 142 and unit_conversion 158 → 182. The 53 rows split cleanly:

32 are a float-comparison bug, not a metric disagreement. eval_sft_tinker_train_csv.py's is_correct() writes abs(pred − truth) <= 1e-2 with no epsilon, and in float64 abs(85.02 − 85.03) evaluates to 0.010000000000005. Rows exactly 0.01 apart — precisely the ones that check was written to accept — fail it. One-line fix in that script, independent of which tolerance is correct.

21 are the genuine absolute-vs-relative gap. That check uses an absolute 1e-2; the competition's stated metric is relative 1e-2, so rows like 152.87 against 152.83 (0.026% off) are correct by the real metric and rejected here.

The 37.5% headline is kept as the CSV reports it, so this page agrees with the numbers already recorded in CLAUDE.md. A grader fixing both issues would print 709 / 40.5% — or 713 / 40.7% if it also applied the tolerance uniformly to equation_numeric's plain-string answers, which it should not (note 2).

Unbalanced step tags are not just truncation. Of the 331 completions with mismatched <step>/</step> counts, 159 stopped cleanly and still came out unbalanced — cipher alone contributes 80 of those. So the model does drop closing tags mid-trace, most often in the categories whose traces are longest. Unbalanced does not imply wrong: 46 of the 331 were still correct. cipher is also the only category emitting tags outside the six-tag vocabulary, and only twice (match, answer) — an inherited quirk of its gold traces, which already use deduction and input_parsing.

Truncation

228 completions hit the token cap. Not one of them was correct.

Across every category, stop_reason == "length" and a right answer never co-occur — 0 for 228. Truncation is therefore a clean write-off rather than a confound: raising the cap can only recover rows the model would have finished, and capping lower costs nothing on rows it already gets right. bit_manipulation is the extreme case: 94 of 250 samples ran out of budget, and its median completion (7,402 tokens) sits within 12% of the ceiling.

Completion length

The column that does real work downstream is longest correct: no right answer in this eval has ever been longer than that, which is what makes it the honest floor for a GRPO max_tokens. For the two categories GRPO currently defaults to, that floor is 759 (numeral) and 1,545 (equation_numeric) — the basis for Cfg.max_tokens = 2048.

equation_numeric is the sharpest shape in the table: a 489-token median against an 8,412-token p95. Its traces are short when the model commits and runaway when it doesn't — the same bimodality that puts 32 of its samples into the cap.

What this says for stage 3

Read as a GRPO planning document rather than a scorecard, the table says one thing loudly: the two categories the GRPO script currently defaults to are the two worst available picks. numeral at 90.0% is close enough to the ceiling that a group of eight rollouts will usually agree; equation_numeric at 9.6% is close enough to the floor that a group will usually agree in the other direction. Either way the centred advantage is near zero and the gradient is noise.

unit_conversion (63.2%), cipher (53.2%) and gravity (45.2%) sit where disagreement actually lives — at roughly 4× the rollout cost, since their traces run 4–5k tokens against numeral's 325. That tradeoff is the decision this eval exists to inform, and it should be confirmed with --probe-headroom rather than settled from this page: these numbers are single-sample at temperature 0 on train.csv, whereas GRPO samples eight times at nonzero temperature on in-distribution synth_sft.jsonl rows and will score higher.

cryptarithm (0.8%) and bit_manipulation (0.4%) are not GRPO problems at all at this accuracy. They need a better policy first — more SFT signal, or a task decomposition — before reinforcement learning has anything to amplify.

Notes

  1. Official rule. Re-grade of model_answer against ground_truth with scripts/eval_train_csv.compare_answer() — exact string, else relative 1e-2 (rel_tol=1e-2, abs_tol=1e-5), with binary strings held to exact match so leading zeros can't be discarded by float parsing. "— same" means the re-grade changed nothing for that category.
  2. equation_numeric is deliberately excluded from the re-grade. Applying the relative tolerance there nominally adds 4 rows, but all four are spurious: three are unrelated integers one apart (104 vs 103, 127 vs 128, 118 vs 119) and the fourth is 2 against a ground truth of 02. equation_numeric answers are plain strings, not estimated decimals, so exact match is the right grader — the same reason binary strings are special-cased.
  3. gravity, 228 conclusion tags but 227 closed </think>. Not a transcription error: one completion emits a conclusion step without ever closing its <think> block. The Valid column requires all four checks and correctly reads 227.
  4. Single-observation cells. cryptarithm got 2 rows right and bit_manipulation 1, so "longest correct" for those is one or two completions, not a distribution. The same caveat applies to their clean-stop accuracy (2/240 and 1/156).
